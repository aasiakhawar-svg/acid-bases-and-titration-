1 · Acids, alkalis and the pH scale

Acids form hydrogen ions (H⁺) in water. Alkalis form hydroxide ions (OH⁻). Slide the pH and watch the ions and indicators change!

pH 7 · neutral

Universal indicator

Key words: a base neutralises an acid (metal oxides and hydroxides). An alkali is a base that dissolves in water to give OH⁻. Neutralisation: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. Litmus only changes colour when H⁺ ions are present, so dry HCl gas in an organic solvent does not turn blue litmus red, but HCl in water does.

4 · Virtual titration

25.0 cm³ of NaOH + methyl orange (yellow in alkali, red in acid). Add acid from the burette until the colour just changes. Go fast first (rough titration), then drop by drop!

on a white tile ⬜

Acid added: 0.00 cm³

Flask is yellow (alkaline).



Good practice: pipette for 25.0 cm³ · rough titration first · swirl · white tile · drop by drop near the end-point · repeat until concordant (within 0.20 cm³) · mean uses only concordant results.

5 · Titration calculation (Q10d)

25.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol/dm³ KOH needs a mean 26.50 cm³ H₂SO₄.   2KOH + H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

① Moles KOH = 0.500 × 25.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.0125 mol
② Ratio 2 KOH : 1 H₂SO₄, so moles H₂SO₄ = 0.0125 ÷ 2 = 0.00625 mol
③ Concentration = moles ÷ volume (dm³) = 0.00625 ÷ (26.50 ÷ 1000)
✅ Concentration of H₂SO₄ = 0.236 mol/dm³
Concordant results (Q10c): 27.40, 27.65, 27.30, 27.35. Concordant = 27.40, 27.30, 27.35 (within 0.20 of each other). Mean = (27.40 + 27.30 + 27.35) ÷ 3 = 27.35 cm³. Burette reading: final 32.45, initial 4.15, volume = 28.30 cm³.
